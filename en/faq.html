FAQ
Short answers to common questions about techno-communism.

Is this "central planning"?

This is real-time planning by telemetry — as corporations already do. Walmart manages 10,000+ stores through an ML demand system. Amazon optimizes supply chains with hourly precision. The difference: corporations plan for profit, we plan for needs. The technology is the same. The objective function is different.

Innovation?

Open science + mission-driven funding outperforms rent-seeking marketing. Rewards follow measurable contribution. The internet, GPS, HTTP, Unix — all created by government funding, not the market. Pharmaceutical innovation: 75% of new molecules discovered by government or university labs (Light & Lexchin, 2005). Capital does not innovate — it appropriates.

Privacy?

For citizens — full. For managers — transparency by consent. Asymmetric transparency: those who take power give up privacy. Those who do not — keep it. This is not surveillance. It is a condition of service.

Transition risks?

Start with areas where planning already wins: logistics, power grids, urban flows — with public KPIs. Estonia already manages government registries through blockchain (since 2012). China plans 50+ "smart cities" with central management. The technology is ready. The question is — whom it serves.

Didn't the USSR prove communism doesn't work?

The USSR failed due to information deficit, not planning. Planners did not have real-time data. Today Walmart does. Amazon does. Alibaba does. The information deficit has been eliminated by technology. What has not been eliminated — private ownership of the means of production, which directs technology toward profit, not needs.

What is NPA?

NPA (Net Present Attention) is the cognitive analog of NPV. Marx described the theft of labor in the present. Reaganomics moved theft to future labor. NPA measures the theft of future coherence. See the "NPA 2.0" page for the full treatment.

Why expropriation, not taxation?

Capital evades taxes. Capital buys regulation. Capital cannot survive expropriation. Marx did not propose a tax on factories — he demanded the abolition of private ownership of the means of production. The greed of capital is berserk — it will burn this planet. The only path is a total ban.

What about freedom?

Freedom is not the right to be exploited. Freedom is the absence of exploitation. Under capitalism, you are free to choose which corporation to sell your labor (or coherence) to. Under techno-communism, you are free from selling. Basic goods are guaranteed. Rare ones — by contribution. No one can appropriate your future labor through NPV or your future coherence through probing.
