The Mathematics of Exploitation
The formulas that rule the world. NPV, r > g, and why the future is devalued.

NPV — The Formula for Stealing the Future

Net Present Value (NPV) has been the primary tool for corporate decision-making since the 1980s. The formula takes future cash flows and discounts them to present value:

\( \mathrm{NPV} = \sum_{t=1}^{H} \frac{CF_t}{(1+r)^t} \)

Where CF_t is the cash flow in year t, r is the discount rate, H is the horizon. The higher r, the smaller the contribution of the distant future. At r = 10% (a typical corporate rate), year-20 revenues are discounted 6.7x. Year-50 revenues — 117x. The future is practically devalued to zero.

When a corporation decides whether to lay off workers, it calculates the NPV of wage savings against the NPV of lost production. The future labor of those laid off — their wages, their consumer demand, their taxes — is discounted to zero. In the formula, they do not exist.

r > g — Why Inequality Grows Automatically

\( r > g \Rightarrow \text{inequality grows exponentially} \)

Thomas Piketty (2014) showed: when the return on capital (r) exceeds the rate of economic growth (g), wealth concentrates automatically. Historically, r ≈ 4-5%, g ≈ 1-2%. The 3-4% annual difference is exponential growth of inequality. The capital owner gets richer faster than the economy grows. The worker falls behind.

In the US from 1980 to 2024: incomes of the top 1% grew by 160%, incomes of the bottom 50% — by 0%. This is not an accident. It is the mathematics of r > g, applied through NPV in every corporate decision.

Marx: Surplus Value

\( W = c + v + m \)

Marx's formula: the value of a commodity W equals constant capital c (raw materials, equipment), variable capital v (wages), and surplus value m (the owner's profit). Surplus value is created by the worker's unpaid labor. The worker produces more than they receive. The difference — m — is appropriated by the owner.

Key point: for Marx, the theft occurs in the present. NPV moved the theft to the future. NPA moved it to the cognitive domain. One formula, three stages of exploitation.

Global Debt — The Quantitative Measure of Appropriated Future

  • Global debt (IIF, Q1 2025): $324 trillion — 326% of world GDP.
  • Public debt (IMF, 2024): $102 trillion.
  • Corporate debt: $87 trillion.
  • At r = 5%, servicing $324T = $16.2T/year — 16% of world GDP.

Debt is appropriated future. Every dollar of debt is future labor, already sold. When r > g, that future labor cannot be produced. The system converges to collapse.

NPA: The Cognitive Analog of NPV

NPA (Net Present Attention) applies the same discounting logic to cognitive retention:

\( \text{Engagement} = \sum_{t=1}^{H} \frac{\text{Coherence}_t}{(1+f)^t} \)

Where f is the probe frequency (analog of r). The higher f, the more future coherence is extracted into the present. D — the stretching exponent — measures extraction intensity. D < 1 is natural retention. D > 1 is extracted.
