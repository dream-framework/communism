Death of Theft of Future
Stopping the financial time machine. Capital appropriates the future — we must take it back.

Abstract

Modern capitalism has evolved — from exploiting present labor to systematically appropriating the future. Marx described the theft of surplus value in the present. Reaganomics of the 1980s turned that theft into appropriation of future labor through NPV. Today, global debt has reached $324 trillion (IIF, Q1 2025) — a quantitative measure of the appropriated future. This work reveals the mechanism of the financial time machine and demonstrates the inevitability of systemic crisis.

1. Evolution of Exploitation

Classical 19th-century capitalism (per Marx): surplus value is created and appropriated in one production cycle. The worker labors today, the owner takes part of the result today. The theft is instantaneous. In the late 20th century, capital learned to monetize future flows in advance — through NPV, which takes future revenues and discounts them to present value. The future labor of the worker becomes a number on a spreadsheet. It is appropriated before it is produced.

Marx in Capital Vol. 3 (1894, Ch. 29-31) described fictitious capital — financial claims on future production. He considered them a deviant form. Reaganomics turned the deviation into the norm. Today, fictitious capital is the only form of capital. Real production is an appendage to the financial machine.

2. The Financial Time Machine

\( \mathrm{NPV} = \sum_{t=1}^{H} \frac{CF_t}{(1+r)^t} \)

Where CF_t are flows created by future labor; r is the discount rate. When a corporation applies NPV to a decision about laying off workers, it discounts the future labor of those laid off to present value and compares it with savings on wages. If savings exceed — they fire. This is credit against the future wages of society, issued by capital to itself.

Piketty showed: when r > g (return on capital exceeds economic growth), inequality grows automatically. The discount rate systematically devalues future social needs. The distant horizon of public needs — healthcare, education, ecology — is underfunded because its NPV approaches zero.

3. The AI Bubble as Diagnosis

The market is capitalizing the right to future revenues from "AI labor," not current returns. AI infrastructure spending in 2025 exceeded $500 billion (Goldman Sachs, Morgan Stanley). Actual AI product revenues — single-digit billions. The gap between valuations and facts is growing. This is a classic bubble of fictitious capital: claims on future production, detached from real production.

  • NVIDIA: $3+T market cap at ~$60B revenue — 50:1 ratio.
  • OpenAI: $157B valuation at ~$4B revenue — 39:1 ratio.
  • Circular deals: AI companies buy cloud capacity from each other, inflating "revenue."

4. The Mathematics of Collapse

\( \lim_{H\to\infty} \sum_{t=1}^{H} \frac{\text{Future labor}}{(1+r)^t} = 0,\quad \text{if } r>g. \)

The system requires eternal growth in returns, but the real economy cannot grow infinitely. When r > g, the time machine breaks: the future labor that capital has already appropriated cannot be produced. Debt reaches unfundable levels. Global debt in 2025: $324 trillion (IIF). Public debt: $102 trillion (IMF, 2024). These numbers are the quantitative measure of the appropriated future.

5. Three Limits

  • Physical: Resources are finite. The planet cannot sustain infinite growth. Humanity's ecological footprint exceeds Earth's biocapacity by 75% (GFN, 2024).
  • Social: Debt and inequality destroy the fabric of society. The richest 1% own 46% of global wealth (Credit Suisse, 2024). US Gini coefficient: 0.49, Russia: 0.41.
  • Mathematical: Debt mass reaches unfundable levels. When r > g, the future labor backing the debt approaches zero. The system converges to collapse.

6. The Bifurcation Point

Techno-feudalism or Techno-communism. The first — capital retains control, digital panopticon tightens, cognitive extraction (NPA) accelerates. The second — the means of production (including means of extraction) are expropriated, real-time planning serves needs, not profit. The only path to survival is a total ban on private ownership of the means of production.
